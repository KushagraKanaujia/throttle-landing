Same GPU. Same model. Same work. One config costs 68.6% less per token.

Throttle measured two vLLM settings on one NVIDIA A100 80GB PCIe, six times in counterbalanced order, and verified the difference. It does not make the GPU faster. It tells you, with a confidence interval, which setting is cheaper and by how much.

Throttle labels this outcome candidate_higher_throughput in golden.json; at a fixed hourly rate higher throughput is the same thing as lower $/M. max_num_seqs=1 is a deliberately simple baseline for a first real-GPU test. Results apply to this model, engine, GPU and workload only.

What this means for your bill

Same tokens, about a third of the GPU.

Pick a monthly volume of output tokens. Monthly GPU cost = tokens ÷ 1,000,000 × the measured $/M for each config.

Arithmetic on this measured workload, not a promise about yours. Your model, prompts, load and GPU change $/M, which is why Throttle measures your stack instead of quoting ours.

How you get this on your stack

throttle check before and after each change.

Three checks to measure your noise, change one setting, check again. Throttle prints $/M tokens with a 95% interval and CHEAPER, MORE EXPENSIVE or NO WINNER, and says NOT CALIBRATED until it has enough repeats. For a decision-grade A/B, throttle golden runs the six-position protocol shown above.

Get Pro early access → Throttle on GitHub 
# 1. today's config, 3 times, changing nothing (noise)
$ for i in 1 2 3; do
    throttle check --url http://localhost:8000 \
      --model Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct \
      --gpu-hourly-rate 1.39 --label before
  done
# 2. restart vLLM with --max-num-seqs 8, then:
$ throttle check --url http://localhost:8000 \
    --model Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct \
    --gpu-hourly-rate 1.39 --label after \
    --config max_num_seqs=8 --monthly-tokens 1B
