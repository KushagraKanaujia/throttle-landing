How the pilot works

How we calculate this
  1. Monthly GPU bill = GPUs × price per GPU-hour × 730 hours (24 × 365 ÷ 12).
  2. Paying for idle = bill × (1 − utilization). Your own arithmetic: the share of paid hours your GPUs spend not generating.
  3. Idle you could reclaim. We don’t assume you can reach 100% busy. We assume you can reach a 70% utilization target, so the same work needs utilization ÷ 70% of today’s GPU-hours. Reclaimable = bill × (1 − utilization ÷ 70%). At or above 70%, this is $0.
  4. Minimum GPUs for your model. Weight memory = parameters × bytes per parameter (FP16/BF16 = 2, FP8 = 1, INT4 = 0.5). We let weights use at most 80% of GPU memory and leave the rest for KV cache and activations. Our measured Qwen2.5-72B run fit on one 192 GB MI300X this way. GPUs needed = weight memory ÷ (80% × memory per GPU), rounded up to 1, 2, 4 or 8 (tensor-parallel splits), or to whole 8-GPU nodes past that.
  5. Over-provisioning (estimate). Only counted if you run at least twice the minimum, so you could split into smaller copies of the model. On Qwen2.5-72B on rented AMD MI300X, going from 1 GPU to 2 gave 1.56× the throughput, so 2 GPUs cost 28% more per token ($2.14 vs $1.67 per million output tokens). So per-token cost on the smaller setup is about 1.56 ÷ 2 = 0.78 of the larger one. We apply that once, even if you could halve more than once, and only to the spend left after reclaiming idle: saving = (bill − reclaimable idle) × 22%. Your number depends on your traffic and latency targets, so Throttle measures it.
  6. On the table = reclaimable idle + over-provisioning, per month; × 12 per year. Never more than your bill, never below $0.
  7. Price per million tokens (if you enter tokens per day) = monthly bill ÷ (tokens per day × 730 ÷ 24) × 1,000,000. “With the waste removed” uses the bill minus the monthly figure above.

What this does not include: engine flags, batching, quantization and engine versions. Those can move cost per token too, in either direction. FP8 on our 72B looked 47% cheaper, but answers ran about 28% longer and nearly all hit the 256-token cap; on the 32B, FP8 answered “!!!!”. That is why Throttle checks output before a change counts.
